We started at 12:16. Not 10:00, the way it said in the plan I'd been refining for the better part of a year — 12:16, after my friend's bus simply never showed up, and after we admitted there was no dignified way to wrestle two roughly 20 kg packs onto public transport and just called a cab to the trailhead. That's how my first-ever multi-day hike began last August: two hours behind schedule, slightly embarrassed, and buzzing anyway, at the edge of Marlay Park with everything I'd need for three days strapped to my back. We'd planned for three days. We did it in two.

A year in the making

This trip was a year in the planning, and you could see it in my Amazon order history. Tucked into an order for phone chargers and dish soap, there'd be a camping trowel. After that, I started judging every purchase by a single question: is this camping-compatible? A year of buying the trip one small, justifiable item at a time — and then, with all that patience, I left the big three until the very last minute.

  1. All year

    Sneaking gear into Amazon orders

    The trowel was the first sneak-in — the moment camping went from "someday" to a thing I was quietly assembling, one order at a time.

  2. The week before

    Panic-buying the big three

    A year of trowels, and the tent, sleeping bag, and pad — the things I'd actually sleep in — came down to a last-week scramble. I also paid for an AllTrails subscription just for this trip.

Day 1 — the chaotic start

Marlay Park → Glencree ~20 km before dark started 12:16
  1. 10:00 (the plan)

    The start that wasn't

    We were supposed to be moving by 10:00. Instead I watched a bus-tracker insist a bus was "due" while my friend stood at a stop where no bus came.

  2. 12:16

    Marlay Park, finally

    Fully loaded, the packs made the decision for us — too heavy to haul across the city by transit, so a cab to the trailhead it was. Not the rugged opening montage I'd pictured. The mood didn't dip for a second.

  3. Afternoon

    Into Glencree — and out of water

    I'd carried 3 litres each in Decathlon bladders. I drank all of it, by midday. We refilled on the shore of the Glencree river and filtered with a Sawyer Mini — a great filter with a maddening workflow: squeezing water into a wide-mouth bladder was slow, fiddly, and left my hands wet and my patience thin.

  4. Evening

    Making camp, the hard way

    We pitched in the woods near the river. The ground was weeds, cones, and sharp sticks, and finding a patch flat and dry enough to sleep on was real graft. The tent, at least, went up without drama — that living-room practice run paid for itself in fading light.

  5. ~21:30

    Wet-wood fire and 9:30 Maggi

    My friend gathered the dampest wood in Wicklow; it took many tries to light. Dinner was Maggi on the stove at around 9:30 pm, possibly the best Maggi of my life — the bar for camp food is gloriously low. Then I crawled into the bag and slept like a baby.

Three litres each, gone by midday. The hills don't care how confident you felt at the trailhead.

Day 2 — the perfect pitch

Glencree → Glendalough scenic high routes tired legs, 20 kg
  1. Early

    Two dogs and a revelation

    I woke to two dogs playing right outside the tent — lovely, until you realise dogs mean people, and people mean a proper pitch was nearby all along. A few steps away: flat, clear ground and a real fire ring someone had already built. Everything we'd fought for the night before, sitting there ready.

  2. Morning

    The plan: just double it

    We'd knocked out around 20 km in half a day on day one, so we figured we'd simply double it — reach Glendalough by evening and catch the first bus back to Dublin. Energy bars for breakfast, off as early as we could.

  3. Midday

    Chasing the scenic routes

    Then AllTrails started whispering. Every scenic alternative — a high route with a better view — we took. More distance, more elevation, more climbing on legs no longer running on adrenaline. And every single time, it was worth it.

What was on my back

Roughly 20 kg each, before food and water (and a litre of water is a kilo, which adds up fast). Here's the kit that went in — tick along as you pack your own. Note the one line at the bottom I drew in the wrong place.

The loadout0 / 12 packed

The merino socks were the single best decision of the trip — dry, warm, no hotspots, two long days in. And then the regret: I left my Crocs / camp shoes at home to save ~350 g, deciding they'd be "too much." Reader, they would not have been too much. They're even in the photo I took of all my gear laid out before I left — they just never made it into the pack.

What I'd do differently

If I could pack a note into the top of that 20 kg bag before 12:16, here's what it would say.

  1. Bring the camp shoes. Always. The weight you save isn't worth the feet you punish at the end of a 20 km day.
  2. Merino socks are non-negotiable. Cheapest happiness per gram on the whole trip.
  3. Pitch early. Daylight buys you the good ground and the fire ring someone already built. Roll in at dusk and you get weeds and a humbling view in the morning.
  4. Sort out water. Carry more than you think, and find a filter workflow that doesn't fight you when you're refilling a bladder.
  5. Test-pitch the tent at home. Ten minutes on the living-room floor saves thirty in a dark, uneven field.
  6. Pay for AllTrails. Take the scenic route. The views you earn with an extra climb are the ones that stay with you.
